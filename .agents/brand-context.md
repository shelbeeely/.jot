# Brand Context

The foundation every brand document reads first. Drawn from the project's own decisions (`DECISIONS.md`), the logo brief
(`docs/proposals/logo.md`), the brand guide (`docs/brand/README.md`) and the site; the owner's choices are marked as such.
Unknown means nobody has decided or measured it yet: don't fill it with a guess.

## Brand
- **Name**: Jot. Written "Jot" in text; the logo spells it "ιot" with a Greek iota (owner's choice). Files are `.jot`, `.jotz`
  (with attachments) and `.jotlock` (locked).
- **Category**: Journaling and note-taking: a plain-text journal format with free tools (a website, a desktop app, a command
  line and a VS Code extension).
- **Description**: Jot things down. Get beautiful pages back.
- **Stage**: Early. A draft format (Jot v1, spec 1.0.0-draft.3, parser 0.1.0); the website is live; the desktop app is built
  from source; no public release of the app or extension yet.
- **Website**: https://shelbeeely.github.io/.jot/ (repository: https://github.com/shelbeeely/.jot)

## Audience
- **Primary Audience** (owner's choice): journal keepers first: diarists, planners, bullet journalers, gratitude and mood
  trackers, people coming back to a notebook after a gap; many are not technical. Second: writers and developers who like
  plain text and files they own. The research behind the tools also serves neurodivergent journal keepers (ADHD, autistic,
  AuDHD) and people tracking health (`docs/methods/`).
- **Key Problem**: Journals last decades and apps don't. Notebook apps keep writing in their own format, often behind an
  account or a subscription, and a method (a bullet journal, morning pages, a mood tracker) usually means a separate app or
  template. Plain text survives but looks bare.
- **Their Language**: Unknown from the audience itself: there are no interviews, reviews or feedback yet. The method names
  people search for are known ("bullet journal", "morning pages", "gratitude", "habit tracker", "Year in Pixels", "five-year
  diary", "spoon theory": `docs/methods/README.md`).

## Positioning
- **Differentiation**: A journal that stays an ordinary text file and still looks like a notebook: dates in words, tasks,
  margin notes, handwriting on a dot grid, charts of your numbers. No account, nothing uploaded, nothing that stops opening.
  Dozens of journaling methods in one file, none of them owning it. Your past online (social exports) can come in too.
- **Competitors**: Day One and similar journaling apps (polished, account and sync based); Obsidian and Markdown note apps
  (local files you own, but not journal-shaped); Notion (cloud workspace); paper notebooks such as Moleskine, Leuchtturm and
  Hobonichi (beautiful, not searchable or durable digitally). These are the category as the project knows it, not a
  researched competitor analysis.
- **Market Position**: Niche and free: a format and open tools, not a paid app. Pricing: none.

## Brand Personality
- **Personality Words** (owner's choice): warm, handmade, calm, quiet. Avoid: corporate or technical, loud or gamified, cute
  or childish, clinical.
- **Tone**: Casual and plain, gently serious: kind about journaling, honest about evidence, never pushy.
- **Voice Admires**: Unknown (not stated).

## Values & Mission
- **Core Values**: Your words are yours (plain files, no account, nothing uploaded). Built to last (the file outlives the
  tools). Permission, not pressure (no streaks or missed-day counts; "just one thought" at a time). Honest about evidence (no
  therapeutic or productivity claims beyond the research). Room for every kind of mind (methods, accessibility, low-demand
  options).
- **Mission** (working draft): Give everyone a journal that is theirs for life: written one thought at a time, in plain text
  that any editor opens, laid out as beautiful pages.
- **The name**: "Jot" comes from iota, the smallest letter; a jotting is a quick note. It also reads as *just one thought*
  (owner's choice): one thought at a time, as brief or as detailed as it needs to be. The logo's stroke and dot are a 1 and a
  0: ten, iota's value, two in binary.
