# Journaling methods research

The guides in this folder are the research behind Jot's profiles, templates, the writer and the format. They come from
[shelbeeely/Journaling `docs/journaling/methods`](https://github.com/shelbeeely/Journaling/tree/main/docs/journaling/methods)
(snapshot and differences: `specification/source-register.md`), plus guides added here. Each is CC0 (`LICENSE`); new guides
follow `TEMPLATE.md`. This README is Jot's own: it is not part of the upstream library.

## Using the research in decisions

The guides total about 36,000 lines, so read this index first, then open the guides that bear on the change. Search the
full guides (`grep -n -i <topic> docs/methods/*.md`) rather than only their opening sections.

1. **Before deciding**, consult the research for any change to a method profile, template, prompt or invitation, and for
   anything touching privacy, sharing, health or mood data, safety, reminders and motivation, accessibility, or tone.
2. **Cite it.** In `DECISIONS.md` entries and `docs/proposals/`, cite the guides that shaped the decision as
   `docs/methods/<file>.md, line N`, quoting briefly where wording matters.
3. **Say where research is thin or disagrees.** Keep each guide's own evidence wording (evidenced, supported, claimed,
   uncertain, unconfirmed). If no guide covers the question, say so; don't present a design choice as a research finding.
4. **Don't claim effects.** Jot records what people write; it makes no therapeutic or productivity claims of its own
   (spec §13 note, `specification/source-register.md`).
5. **Fix the research where it's wrong.** Corrections to a guide go upstream as well; note local differences in the
   source register.

## Principles that recur across the guides

These are the findings most often relevant to Jot decisions. Each names example sources; the guides hold the detail.

- **Private by default; sharing is a separate, deliberate choice.** expressive-writing.md 443, 602; gratitude-journaling.md
  602; autistic-sensory-journaling.md 691 (summaries only, by choice).
- **Plan for the journal being found.** Code words, neutral covers, nothing that harms if read.
  gender-euphoria-and-hrt-log.md 497, 606; dbt-diary-card.md 684.
- **Gaps are data, not failures.** Leave missed days blank; don't reconstruct or count them. symptom-tracking.md 596;
  low-demand-check-in.md 89, 236.
- **No streaks, catch-up prompts or shame wording by default.** A broken streak shown prominently can lower engagement.
  habit-tracking.md 155; low-demand-check-in.md 183, 236.
- **Record; don't diagnose or score.** The writer or their clinician interprets. symptom-tracking.md 683;
  autistic-sensory-journaling.md 15.
- **Some methods are built for use with a clinician or trusted person**, and say so: DBT cards, thought records, safety
  plans, trauma writing. dbt-diary-card.md 16, 215; cbt-thought-record.md 244; safety-planning.md 52, 176;
  expressive-writing.md 440.
- **Crisis-adjacent pages carry a short, real pointer to help**, not alarms. dbt-diary-card.md 723; safety-planning.md 207.
- **Colour is never the only signal.** design-iteration-and-critique.md 184; moodboard-and-palette.md 180;
  symptom-tracking.md 419.
- **Measure only what answers a question you have.** symptom-tracking.md 110; habit-tracking.md 37.
- **Low demand works.** A line, a number or "skipped" is a complete entry. low-demand-check-in.md 15;
  five-year-diary.md 538.
- **Rereading has value, on the writer's terms.** five-year-diary.md 50, 441, 452; commonplace-book.md 204.
