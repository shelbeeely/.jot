# Name of the method (creator, if any)

One or two sentences: what this doc covers and when it was checked. Mark anything you could not confirm from a primary source with **(unconfirmed)**.

## 1. At a glance

A two-column table: creator, year, type, time per day and week, cost, formats, best for, not ideal for, learning curve, supplies, official site, evidence level. Facts only.

## 2. Summary

Five bullets or a short paragraph: what it is, how it works, why it exists, where it went.

## 3. History and origin

Who made it, when, a dated timeline, the cultural context. Cite every date.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

The ideas the method rests on, in the creator's terms and in plain words.

## 5. Core components

Each named part of the method and what it is for.

## 6. Setting it up

First setup, first day, first week. Steps someone new can follow.

## 7. Daily practice

What a normal day looks like, with times.

## 8. Rhythms

Weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly routines, including review and what to do when you miss days.

## 9. Page anatomy

What goes where on each page type. ASCII sketches are welcome.

## 10. Worked examples

A day, a week and a month, with invented sample content only.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

How people adapt it, and which variants are worth knowing.

## 12. Community practice

Who uses it, where people share it, common habits. No personal data or named private individuals.

## 13. Official products and formats

Official notebooks, apps and templates, with prices and dates.

## 14. Tools and supplies

What to buy or use, including low-cost and accessible options.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

Apps, e-ink, scanning, and what is lost or gained.

## 16. Evidence and research

What studies exist, what they found, how strong they are. Name the evidence level and say why. Health claims need peer-reviewed or clinical sources.

Separate evidence for the exact method or log from related treatments, learning techniques, mechanisms or community accounts.
For key findings, give the studied population, intervention, outcome and limitation on applying it here. Qualitative evidence
can support descriptions of experience; it is not an outcome trial. A creator's instructions establish method details, not
efficacy. Label original Jot adaptations and design choices explicitly. Do not infer that an untested practice is low risk.

| Claim | Source and what was studied | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A specific, limited finding | [1], population, intervention and measured outcome | Limits of applying it to this journal format |

## 17. Benefits

Benefits that the sources support, each tied to a source or marked as reported by practitioners.

## 18. Pitfalls

How it goes wrong, and how to recover.

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

Vision, motor, cognitive, sensory, energy and time limits. Concrete adaptations, not generalities.

## 20. Comparison

A table against two to four nearby methods.

## 21. Combining

How it pairs with other methods.

## 22. Ready-to-use bank

Prompts, headings, symbols or checklists a reader can copy.

## 23. Glossary

Terms in alphabetical order.

## 24. FAQ

Real questions, short answers.

## 25. For Jot

What the text format, Writer, template editor and paper export can take from the method. Distinguish suggested design from
verified implementation. Check current Jot guides before describing a feature as available. Dedicated hardware is deferred.
For an existing profile, preserve its fields and semantics from specification/profiles.json; examples must parse as Jot.
Inherited guides may retain their original "For Journalwright Studio" heading; its product plans are not Jot feature claims.

## 26. Open questions

What is unknown, unconfirmed or disputed. Mark unconfirmed details (unconfirmed) in the text.

## 27. Further reading

Books and articles that were not used as sources but are worth a look.

## 28. Sources

Checked YYYY-MM-DD: name the sources and claims actually reviewed. For an inherited bibliography, preserve its earlier date
and identify the subset rechecked. Say when only an abstract or bibliographic record was accessible, or when access failed.
Do not claim an exhaustive search or an all-source check after a targeted update.

Then a numbered list, one source per line: title, author or publisher, publication date, URL. Open every new source before
you list it. Keep reference numbers unique and cite them beside the claim. Date prices, software features and compatibility.
