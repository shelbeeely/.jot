# Free diary and stream of consciousness

A free diary keeps whatever the writer wants to put into words, without a prescribed length, topic or ending. This guide covers Jot's optional `free-diary@1` label; ordinary prose works without it [1].

Sources checked 2026-11-02, with access limits in section 28. No study reviewed here tests this exact profile. Research about structured emotional disclosure is not evidence that every form of diary writing has the same effects.

## 2. Summary

- Start with what you want to write, including "I don't know what to write."
- Dates, headings, prompts, mood labels and conclusions are optional.
- An unfinished sentence can remain unfinished.
- A record is not an obligation to reread, improve or share it.
- Use another method only when its structure answers a need you have.

## 3. History and origin

There is no single founding event claimed for the broad practice. This guide does not assign one person's writing system to all diaries.

| Date | Relevant development, not a lineage claim |
| --- | --- |
| 2003 | Bolger, Davis and Rafaeli review diary methods as tools for studying everyday experience [2] |
| 2022 | Sohal and colleagues review clinical journaling interventions, largely structured forms of writing [3] |
| 2026 | Jot includes `free-diary@1` to label unrestricted reflection when an application wants an explicit method [1] |

"Stream of consciousness" here means following the next thought in everyday writing. It does not claim that unedited prose is a complete or unfiltered record of the mind.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

The writer chooses the subject and the stopping point. A sentence does not have to become a lesson, a goal or an explanation.

- Keep the version that was true to the writing moment.
- Allow two entries to disagree.
- Describe uncertainty rather than repairing it with invented detail.
- Let privacy determine what is safe to record.
- Treat stopping, changing the subject and leaving a gap as choices.

## 5. Core components

The structured Jot profile is deliberately small [1].

| Field | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `body` | Required only for a structured reflection record: the writing itself |
| `date` | Optional date supplied by the writer |
| `title` | Optional heading, including a neutral one |

Core prose does not require a record block or a date. The profile is a label for applications, not a reason to turn an ordinary paragraph into a form.

## 6. Setting it up

1. Choose somewhere you can reopen and edit without difficulty.
2. Decide who might see it. A plain file or notebook is readable to anyone with access.
3. Write one sentence, an item you noticed, or a fragment.
4. Stop when you want to stop.

For the first week, keep only the parts that help you return. No page allocation or backlog is necessary. If a blank page is difficult, borrow one prompt from section 22 and leave the rest.

## 7. Daily practice

There is no required daily practice. An optional short session could be:

- Notice what you want to keep.
- Write it in the order it arrives.
- Close the page without evaluating the entry.

Rereading and editing are separate options. If you want a raw record and a polished version, keep them separately so later edits do not silently replace what you originally wrote.

## 8. Rhythms

| Rhythm | Optional use |
| --- | --- |
| During a day | Catch a phrase before it disappears |
| Weekly | Give one entry a useful title, or leave everything as it is |
| Monthly | Check that the storage and privacy arrangements still suit you |
| Yearly | Preserve a copy in a format you can open; decide whether any writing should be kept, removed or shared |

After a gap, begin with today or no date at all. Reconstructing missing days is optional; label a later recollection as such rather than pretending it was written then.

## 9. Page anatomy

```text
Optional date or heading

Whatever I want to write.
A fragment. Another thought that does not quite agree.

Optional note added later, labelled as later.
```

White space can be useful. There is no minimum page coverage and no box that has to be filled before the entry counts.

## 10. Worked examples

All people and situations below are invented. These examples show choices, not a schedule to follow.

**A day, as ordinary prose:**

```text
The bus window kept fogging. I drew a small circle to see the river.
I thought I wanted a quiet evening, but now I want company.
```

**A week, with an optional label:**

```jot
:::record reflection #rainy-window
method: free-diary@1
title: A small view
body: I kept thinking about that circle in the bus window. I do not know why.
:::
```

The rest of the week can remain blank. Nothing in that record asks for a mood score, an explanation or another entry.

**A month:** the writer reads two entries, leaves the others closed, and adds: "Later note: I eventually remembered the river's name." The original words stay intact. A correction to a fact can be labelled without making the older feeling wrong.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

| Variation | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| One-line diary | A deliberate small space, without requiring one entry every day |
| Unsent letter | An imagined recipient; sending remains a separate decision |
| Stream-of-consciousness page | Follow associations without a planned outline |
| Observation diary | Concrete details rather than interpretation |
| Voice-first diary | Dictate through a chosen tool, then review what was transcribed |

Morning Pages and expressive writing have their own instructions. A free diary does not inherit their page counts, session lengths or proposed effects.

## 12. Community practice

Diaries may be private, kept for an archive or selected for publication. These are different audiences. Public diary examples are edited and self-selected; they do not define what a private entry should look like.

If borrowing a prompt from someone else, credit it when relevant and check whether it invites more disclosure than you want. There is no need to post a page to participate in the practice.

## 13. Official products and formats

There is no required official notebook, app, subscription or certification. Jot's original profile is documented in the local profile catalogue [1].

Commercial diaries may offer useful paper or bindings, but purchasing one is not a condition of writing freely. Prices are omitted because no particular product is required or compared here.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- Use spare paper, an existing notebook or a plain-text file.
- Choose a comfortable pen, keyboard, switch input or dictation tool.
- A neutral cover or filename may help where visibility matters.
- Keep a copy you can open independently of the current app if long-term access matters.

A timer is optional. It can provide a stopping point; it need not impose a minimum writing time.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

Digital writing allows search, larger text and editing. Paper can be quick to open and does not need a battery. Neither medium guarantees privacy or a better psychological outcome.

Photographs, transcription and cloud backup create additional copies. Choose them deliberately. A cropped screenshot may still contain identifying details, and a visible page is not made private by calling it a draft.

If dictating, check where audio and transcripts go. Treat recognition errors as transcription errors, not remembered facts. Keep the writer's uncertainty when reviewing the text.

## 16. Evidence and research

Evidence level: **anecdotal for unrestricted free diary writing as described here**. The checked research is adjacent, not a trial of `free-diary@1`.

| Source | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bolger et al. 2003 [2] | Frequent reports can capture daily experiences for research; a review of measurement designs | That keeping a private diary improves health, or that every entry is accurate |
| Sohal et al. 2022 [3] | Some clinical journaling interventions showed small-to-moderate benefits, with high heterogeneity and methodological limitations | The same effect for unstructured writing, any individual writer or the Jot profile |
| Berkeley's Time Capsule practice [4] | A research-informed activity for recording and later revisiting ordinary experiences | A controlled test of an ongoing free diary or a requirement to resurface personal writing |

The Sohal review included 20 randomised trials across different mental-health presentations. Its clinical framing and instructions should not be replaced with the claim "any diary is therapy."

Absence of a trial of this exact format does not mean the practice has no personal value. It means effects, optimal frequency and harms for this format remain uncertain.

## 17. Benefits

- It keeps words that can be reopened later: a property of keeping a record.
- It allows a writer to choose subjects and length: a design choice.
- Ordinary records may become interesting to revisit [4], but resurfacing should remain optional.
- Some writers describe relief or clarity; these are possible experiences, not promised outcomes of this profile.

## 18. Pitfalls

| Difficulty | A possible response |
| --- | --- |
| Turning the diary into a performance | Keep a private draft, or write less |
| Repeating a distressing loop | Change subject, stop or seek support if needed |
| Feeling obliged to disclose everything | Leave details out; a record can be partial |
| Rewriting the past to make it coherent | Add a later note rather than replacing the old entry |
| Keeping writing only in a fragile app | Make an export you can reopen |

If writing repeatedly leaves you more distressed or interferes with daily life, pause and consider appropriate support. More disclosure is not automatically better.

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **Vision:** use large type, a screen reader or bold ruling with ample space.
- **Motor effort:** type, dictate or use a short phrase; handwriting is not required.
- **Language:** write in the language or mixture of languages that feels available.
- **Energy:** a word is enough; dates and titles can be omitted.
- **Cognition:** keep a single easy-to-find place, or accept several loose notes without mandatory sorting.
- **Privacy assistance:** if someone helps enter text, agree what they may read and retain first.

These are access choices, not evidence that a particular tool treats an underlying difficulty.

## 21. Combining

Keep a free paragraph beside a structured tracker without treating it as a score explanation. A reading response or memory can begin as ordinary prose and remain that way.

If a specific purpose later needs more structure, add only what helps. The diary does not need to become a complete history of the writer's health, relationships or tasks.

## 22. Ready-to-use bank

- What do I want to write?
- A small thing I noticed was...
- I am unsure about...
- Two things can both be true here...
- I do not want to explain this today.
- Later note, added on ___:

Every prompt is optional. A blank response is not missing data that an application should fill.

## 23. Glossary

| Term | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| Core prose | Ordinary writing without a structured method record |
| Free diary | Writing without imposed topic, length or conclusion |
| Later note | A clearly labelled addition made after the original entry |
| Stream of consciousness | Following successive thoughts without an outline |

## 24. FAQ

**Do I need a date?** No. Add one only if it helps; do not invent a date you do not know [1].

**Should I write every day?** No frequency is required or established as best for this format.

**May I delete an entry?** Yes. Consider any copies, backups or shared versions separately.

**Do I have to write honestly?** You choose what to disclose. Label fiction or imagined detail if it could later be mistaken for a remembered event.

**Does this count as therapy?** The profile records writing. It is not a treatment protocol.

## 25. For Jot

Adopt a clear writing area, optional dates and headings, and the ability to stop without completing a form. Keep ordinary prose first; `free-diary@1` must remain optional [1].

Do not infer mood, require a conclusion, reward disclosure or generate remembered details. Rereading, sharing, reminders and any external processing need explicit choices. A text export should preserve the writing without requiring the originating app.

## 26. Open questions

- Which writers prefer a completely blank page to a small optional invitation?
- When does rereading feel useful, neutral or distressing?
- How do access needs affect the effort of keeping a private diary?
- What outcomes, if any, would a trial of unrestricted writing measure fairly?

These questions are not answered by trials of a different, prescribed writing protocol.
