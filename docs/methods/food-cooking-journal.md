# Food, cooking and meal memories

A food and cooking journal keeps what you made or ate, what you changed and what you want to remember. A recipe experiment, a takeaway meal and a memory of someone's kitchen all belong. This guide documents Jot's original `food-cooking-journal@1` profile; sources were checked on 2026-11-02, with scope recorded in section 28.

> Health note: this is a practical and autobiographical journal, not a diet, allergy diagnosis or food-safety system. It requires no calories, weight, portion targets or moral labels for food. Use current official guidance for safe preparation and storage [2]; a remembered recipe or a pleasant smell cannot establish safety.

## 2. Summary

- Write what you want to remember, rather than everything you ate.
- Link to a recipe or name its source when useful.
- Keep an actual substitution separate from an idea for next time.
- Describe texture, smell, temperature or setting in your own terms.
- Leave gaps. There is no requirement to account for a day or a meal.

## 3. History and origin

Recipe collections, food writing and personal diaries overlap, but they do not form one standardized method. This guide makes no claim to a first recipe notebook or a universal cultural history.

Jot's source register describes food-cooking-journal as an original complementary profile. Its contribution is a small record structure, not a newly discovered culinary practice.

A 2018 systematic review examined psychosocial outcomes of cooking interventions [1]. Those interventions are related context, not the origin or validation of this journal.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

1. **Keep what helps you return.** A change worth repeating can be enough.
2. **Preference is personal.** "Too crunchy for me" describes an experience without judging the food or the person.
3. **Meals need no performance.** Purchased, assembled and prepared meals all qualify.
4. **Memory may be uncertain.** Write "I think" or "approximately" when that is what you know.
5. **A recipe note is not a safety judgment.** Check current guidance separately [2].
6. **People choose what is shared.** A household memory can be private even when a recipe is public.

## 5. Core components

The profile uses `:::record reflection` and `method: food-cooking-journal@1`.

| Field | Required? | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `body` | Yes | What happened, tasted different or felt worth remembering |
| `meal` | No | A meal name or occasion, in the writer's words |
| `recipe` | No | Recipe title, book and page, or a source link |
| `ingredient` | No; repeatable | Ingredients worth noting; no full inventory is required |
| `substitution` | No; repeatable | What actually replaced or changed an ingredient |
| `context` | No | Setting, equipment, company or practical constraints |

These fields hold description, not a computed nutrition model. If nutrient estimates are added elsewhere, the profile requires separate source-labeled measurements; do not invent amounts from prose or a photograph.

## 6. Setting it up

1. Choose one place: a notebook margin, a recipe card or a dated file.
2. Start with the question: "What would I like to remember next time?"
3. Save the recipe source if there is one. A title and page may be easier to keep than a changing link.
4. Add a short note after the meal or when convenient. There is no reporting deadline.
5. After a few entries, keep only the fields you use.

First week: try one recipe-change note and one meal memory, if both interest you. Either alone is enough; these are invitations, not a schedule.

## 7. Daily practice

There is no daily requirement. When a meal leaves something worth keeping:

- Name it, or start straight with the sentence.
- Write the change or observation precisely enough for your future self.
- If you want, add one next-time idea in `body`.
- Close the journal. No rating, photograph or nutritional total is needed.

Example: "The noodles stayed firmer when I dressed them at the table. Try that again." This records an observation, not a controlled experiment or a safety conclusion.

## 8. Rhythms

| Rhythm | Optional practice |
| --- | --- |
| After a meal | One practical note or memory |
| Before shopping | Reread one or two entries you want to repeat |
| Weekly | Keep a short list of meals that fitted the week's circumstances |
| Monthly | Move a useful variation to a recipe card, keeping its source |
| After a gap | Write the next thing worth remembering; no reconstruction |

A review can ask whether the journal is useful. If not, simplify or stop it. No archive needs to be complete.

## 9. Page anatomy

```text
Meal or occasion: __________________
Recipe/source, if any: ______________
What changed: ______________________
What I noticed: ____________________
Next time, if wanted: _______________
```

A small margin note can replace the whole page. Keep identifying details about other people out if the notebook may be shared.

## 10. Worked examples

All content below is invented. It is not preparation, storage or dietary advice.

A day, with two repeatable substitutions:

```jot
### 2026-11-02 | Monday

:::record reflection
method: food-cooking-journal@1
meal: Lunch
recipe: My lentil soup notes, page 12
ingredient: Lentils
ingredient: Carrot
substitution: Used extra carrot in place of celery
substitution: Used parsley instead of dill
context: Quiet lunch at home
body: I preferred the softer texture. Next time I want to write down the herb amount while I am cooking.
:::
```

A week of selective notes:

| Day | Entry | Why it stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Soup variation above | A change to try again |
| Wednesday | "The cafe toast reminded me of a holiday breakfast" | A memory, without a recipe |
| Friday | "An assembled plate fitted the evening" | A practical option for another tired evening |
| Sunday | No entry | Nothing needs filling in |

A month: "The notes I returned to were the soup and the quick assembled meal. I want a small list of easy textures, not a complete food diary." This is the writer's assessment of usefulness, not a claim about health or cooking skill.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

- **Recipe test:** one change at a time when feasible, with the previous version named.
- **Meal memory:** where you were and what the meal brings to mind.
- **Sensory preference note:** a writer-chosen description such as smooth, separate or mild.
- **Household collection:** contributions kept in each person's own words, with permission.
- **No-cook notes:** combinations, purchased options and useful preparation shortcuts.
- **Recipe commonplace:** save a source and your annotation; see [commonplace books](commonplace-book.md).

## 12. Community practice

People exchange recipes in households, classes and public collections. This guide does not infer how common a particular journaling format is from that activity.

For a shared recipe, distinguish the source's instructions from your adaptation. Ask before publishing another person's story, photograph or private recipe. A person may agree to share ingredients and decline to share the surrounding memory.

## 13. Official products and formats

There is no official product for this profile. Blank notebooks and existing recipe cards work.

Commercial recipe organizers may offer shopping lists, nutrition estimates or public sharing. Those features are separate choices; none is required by this method. No product prices or current app capabilities were audited for this guide.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- A writing tool you already have.
- An optional recipe index by title or page.
- Optional photos with a descriptive caption if images help you remember.
- A source link or book reference for a recipe you want to find again.

Cooking equipment depends on the recipe and the writer's circumstances. The journal does not prescribe equipment, ingredient purchases or a spending target.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

Plain text can hold the entire practice. Paper recipe cards can be photographed and described in a journal; the description remains useful without the image.

Keep original wording separate from an adaptation. Links may change, so a title, author or publication date can help you find the source later. Do not copy or redistribute a complete book or paid recipe collection without the relevant permission.

Before sharing a `.jotz`, check attachments as well as visible writing. Photographs can reveal people, addresses or a home. A private journal is not made anonymous by removing names from one field.

## 16. Evidence and research

**Evidence level: anecdotal for the journal.** None of the sources checked here tests this profile, its fields or its review schedule. This was a targeted source pass, not a systematic search establishing that no such studies exist.

| Source and design | What it supports | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Farmer, Touchton-Leonard and Ross 2018 systematic review [1] | Preliminary psychosocial findings from cooking interventions | Eleven included studies; generally weak research quality, nonrandomization, small samples and unvalidated measures; not a cooking-journal trial |
| FoodSafety.gov official guidance [2] | Separate current guidance for clean, separate, cook and chill practices | Does not validate a recipe, substitution or storage decision in a diary |
| NIMH public clinical information [3] | Food-intake preoccupation can be a reason to seek professional help | Does not diagnose someone from a journal or test this profile |

The review's outcomes must not be transferred to a notebook. Cooking classes can include activity, social contact and instruction; a meal-memory entry contains a different set of components.

## 17. Benefits

These are proposed practical uses, not proven effects:

- Find a substitution you want to repeat.
- Keep a sensory preference in your own words.
- Preserve an ordinary meal memory without recording an entire day.
- Leave a recipe annotation that your future self can understand.

This guide does not claim improved nutrition, weight change, symptom control, mental health or reduced food waste from journaling.

## 18. Pitfalls

| Difficulty | Possible adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| The journal becomes an inventory | Keep only the meal or change you want to remember |
| Every meal needs a score | Remove ratings; use descriptive words or stop |
| A substitution is remembered as original | Label it as your adaptation and keep the source |
| A guess becomes an exact quantity | Retain "approximately" or leave the number out |
| A traditional recipe is assumed safe | Check current official guidance separately [2] |
| Writing increases food-related guilt or checking | Pause the journal; discuss persistent concerns with a qualified professional [3] |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **Low energy:** one sentence, a saved recipe title or nothing today.
- **Motor limits:** type or dictate if wanted; avoid keeping devices in a way that interferes with safe kitchen work.
- **Vision:** larger text and clear spacing; label photographs and avoid color-only ratings.
- **Sensory needs:** describe your preference without needing to justify it or challenge it.
- **Limited time or money:** record meals that already fit your circumstances; no special purchase is needed.
- **Shared kitchens:** note the available equipment if useful, without identifying housemates.
- **Memory uncertainty:** "I think" is useful information, not an error to conceal.

## 21. Combining

Use a commonplace index for recipe sources and a dated diary for memories. Either can link to the other without copying every entry.

A sensory note can sit beside [autistic sensory journaling](autistic-sensory-journaling.md), if the writer wants. It need not become a challenge, exposure exercise or diagnosis.

If a clinician requests a specific intake or symptom record, agree its purpose and fields with them. Do not assume that this optional memory journal supplies the information they need.

| Term | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| Adaptation | A change to a source recipe, kept distinct from the original |
| Context | Circumstances that help explain the meal or memory |
| Meal memory | A personal recollection connected with food |

## 24. FAQ

**Must I cook?** No. A bought meal or an assembled snack can be worth remembering.

**Do I list everything I eat?** No. The profile requires only `body`, the sentence you want to keep.

**Can I include numbers?** Yes, if useful and accurate enough for your purpose. Calories and nutrient estimates are not required; separate sourced measurements from memory and guesswork.

**Does a note prove an ingredient caused a symptom?** No. Discuss health questions with a qualified professional; repeated diary associations alone do not establish causation.

**What if a family recipe contains unsafe instructions?** Keep the historical wording clearly labeled if you want the memory, and use current official guidance for actual preparation [2].

## 25. For Journalwright Studio

For Jot, adopt a free-text-first reflection with optional recipe source and repeatable ingredient and substitution fields. Make a one-sentence entry complete. Preserve attribution and uncertainty when importing recipe notes.

Do not add calorie totals, dietary verdicts, inferred allergens, automatic symptom-cause labels or a required meal schedule. Nutrition measurements need their own source. Product defaults here are editorial choices, not demonstrated health effects.
