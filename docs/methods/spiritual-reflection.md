# Spiritual, ritual and meaning journals

A place to write about meaning, prayer, values, ritual or uncertainty. It can accompany a religious practice, a secular
practice, changing beliefs or no named tradition. This guide describes Jot's `spiritual-reflection@1` profile, not a
single historical method. Sources checked 2026-11-02; access limits are recorded in section 28.

> **Care note.** This page does not establish spiritual truths, diagnose experiences or replace mental health care.
> Leave questions unresolved. If reflection brings guilt, fear or pressure, stop or change the practice and consider
> support from someone who respects your beliefs, doubts and boundaries.

## 2. Summary

- Start from what felt meaningful, difficult or worth sitting with.
- Write one `body` field. All other profile fields are optional.
- Name a tradition only if it helps; secular, uncertain and changing positions belong here too.
- Record a ritual as something you did, without inferring supernatural effects.
- Attribute quoted sacred or literary words and keep your response distinct from the quotation.
- A question can remain a question. Sharing and rereading are separate choices.

## 3. History and origin

This is an umbrella recording practice, not a claim that prayer journals, philosophical notebooks and secular rituals
have one origin or mean the same thing. A guide to a particular tradition would need that tradition's own sources.

| Date | Context, not a universal origin story |
| --- | --- |
| 2009 | A consensus conference report treated spiritual care as a dimension of palliative care [2] |
| 2015 | Gonçalves et al. reviewed randomized trials of religious and spiritual interventions in mental health care [3] |
| 2026 | Jot's version 1 catalogue includes `spiritual-reflection@1`; this guide documents its existing fields |

Clinical spiritual care and a private journal have different purposes. Neither clinical history-taking nor an
intervention study establishes a preferred belief system or a universal journaling procedure.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

**The writer supplies the meaning.** A page can hold devotion, gratitude, doubt, anger or an ordinary observation.
There is no required interpretation of what happened.

**Practice and claim are different.** “I lit a candle and felt calmer” records an experience. It does not establish
that a ritual caused the feeling or that the ritual has a supernatural effect.

**Belonging is optional.** VA guidance recognizes spiritual practice with or without religious affiliation [1]. This
profile also allows the writer to reject the word “spiritual” and use “meaning” or “values.” That is an inclusion
choice for Jot, not a definition every tradition must accept.

**Uncertainty can stay.** No prompt requires closure, forgiveness, gratitude or agreement with a community.

## 5. Core components

The record type is `reflection`, with `method: spiritual-reflection@1` identifying the profile.

| Field | Requirement | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `body` | Required | The writer's reflection, however short |
| `tradition` | Optional | A tradition or framework in the writer's words |
| `ritual` | Optional | A practice performed or considered |
| `question` | Optional | Something unresolved; no answer is required |
| `source` | Optional | Attribution or a reference for a text, teaching or other source |

An empty optional field can be omitted. No belief, mood rating, attendance count or spiritual progress score is
required. If quoting more than one source, identify each quotation in the body as well.

## 6. Setting it up

1. Choose a private notebook or file and a neutral title if that protects you.
2. Choose your own opening: “What mattered?”, “A prayer”, “A question” or no heading.
3. Write a sentence before adding any structure. That sentence is a complete first entry.
4. Add a source reference if you are responding to someone else's words.
5. Decide whether rereading would help. Leave it unscheduled if it becomes a demand.

For a first week, use the same small opening a few times if wanted. Do not reconstruct days you did not write.

## 7. Daily practice

Notice one thing: a conversation, an absence, a ceremony, an ordinary act or a question. Describe what happened,
then what it meant to you today. You can stop there.

If a ritual is part of the entry, write what you actually did. It need not involve a purchased object, a flame,
fasting, silence or any action that is unsafe or inaccessible. This guide prescribes no ritual technique.

## 8. Rhythms

- **After an event:** write when a visit, service, walk or change in belief feels worth recording.
- **Weekly, optional:** reread one entry and ask whether anything still matters or needs care.
- **Monthly, optional:** notice changed questions or values without scoring spiritual improvement.
- **After a gap:** begin with today. A missing entry says nothing about commitment or worth.
- **Seasonal or yearly:** follow a calendar only if it belongs to your chosen practice.

## 9. Page anatomy

```text
Meaning or reflection                         date, if wanted

What happened / what I am sitting with:
___________________________________________________________

Optional: ritual / question / source
___________________________________________________________
```

Keep the body largest. A source line distinguishes another person's words from your response. A question box is
optional; a blank box must not look like an unfinished assignment.

## 10. Worked examples

All examples below are invented. They describe individual choices, not standard practice for a community.

**A day, with a secular ritual:**

```jot
### 2026-11-02 | Monday

:::record reflection
method: spiritual-reflection@1
body: Putting the work notebook away gave the evening a boundary. I want to keep that small pause.
ritual: A quiet minute before making dinner
question: What would make rest feel permitted?
:::
```

**A week, with different kinds of entries:**

| Day | Invented note |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | A quiet minute helped me notice I was still thinking about work |
| Thursday | Prayer felt difficult today; I do not need to explain that away |
| Saturday | Helping a neighbor mattered more to me than finding the right words |
| Sunday | No entry; nothing to fill in later |

**A month, with a source and an open question:**

```jot
### 2026-11-30 | Monday

:::record reflection
method: spiritual-reflection@1
body: This month I kept returning to connection. I am less sure which label fits my beliefs, and I can leave that open.
source: VA Whole Health, Spirit and Soul; my response to its discussion of connection, accessed 2026-11-02
question: Who can listen without asking me to decide?
:::
```

The source field names a paraphrased reading, not a quotation. For a direct quotation, mark the quoted words and
record author, title, edition or translation, and page or passage when available.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

- **Prayer journal:** write prayers or responses in your own tradition and language.
- **Secular meaning journal:** keep acts, relationships or values without religious labels.
- **Ritual notebook:** record what a chosen practice involved and how it felt, including “nothing noticeable.”
- **Question notebook:** collect uncertainties without turning them into problems to solve.
- **Changing-belief journal:** preserve old entries without requiring your present self to agree with them.

These are possible uses, not interchangeable descriptions of every religious or secular practice.

## 12. Community practice

Writing may be private, discussed with a trusted person or connected to a community. This guide has not surveyed
communities and makes no claim about how common any format is.

Before a group exercise, agree whether anyone will read the writing, whether passing is allowed and whether notes
will be kept. Participation in a gathering is not consent to share a private entry.

## 13. Official products and formats

No official notebook or paid product is needed for this Jot profile. Tradition-specific publications have their own
authors, permissions and teaching contexts.

Clinical tools are separate. The GWish FICA spiritual history tool is for care conversations [4], not a general
journal template; it has its own copyright and permission terms. This guide does not reproduce or adapt it.

## 14. Tools and supplies

A notebook or ordinary text file is enough. Optional aids include a page marker, large-print reading, a familiar
object or a comfortable place to sit. None is a prerequisite.

Choose a flameless light instead of a candle if wanted. A spoken word or typed line can replace handwriting. Do not
buy ritual supplies to make an entry count.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

A `.jot` entry remains readable text. If a file contains private beliefs or questions, choose its location and
backups deliberately. Plain text is not encrypted merely because it is local.

A paper page can hold source references to digital readings. Before photographing or sharing it, check quotations,
names and references that reveal other people's beliefs or private circumstances.

## 16. Evidence and research

Evidence level: **anecdotal for this recording format**. No direct trial of `spiritual-reflection@1` was identified
in the sources checked for this guide. This was a focused source check, not a systematic search of all journaling
research.

| Source | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VA Whole Health, Spirit and Soul [1] | Clinical practice context for meaning, connection and practices with or without religion | A causal health benefit from this journal or a preferred interpretation |
| Puchalski et al., 2009 [2] | A professional consensus report on spiritual care in palliative care | An intervention effect or a journaling protocol; only the abstract was checked |
| Gonçalves et al., 2015 [3] | A review of 23 randomized trials of diverse religious or spiritual interventions; reported benefits for some outcomes, especially anxiety | That an individual journal produces those effects, or that every belief or ritual is beneficial |
| GWish clinical FICA page [4] | The existence and clinical purpose of a spiritual history tool | Validation of this profile or permission to reproduce the tool |

The review combines different protocols, populations and outcomes and calls for more standardized research [3].
Its findings should not be transferred to this short, optional page. Observed experiences, professional guidance
and trial outcomes are different kinds of evidence.

## 17. Benefits

- A place to preserve personal words and questions: a function of the record, not a demonstrated health effect.
- A reference for a conversation the writer chooses to have: a possible practical use, not an outcome guarantee.
- Space for changing beliefs without deleting the past: a design choice.
- A way to separate a quoted source from a personal interpretation: a documentation aid.

Relief, peace, connection or clarity may be reported by a writer. Their absence does not mean the entry failed.

## 18. Pitfalls

| Pitfall | A possible response |
| --- | --- |
| The page feels like a test of faith or worth | Remove scores, quotas and required conclusions; stop if wanted |
| Repeated checking for the perfect interpretation | Close the page and return to an ordinary activity; seek support if the loop is distressing |
| A question becomes forced gratitude or forgiveness | Record anger, uncertainty or nothing instead |
| A ritual is treated as proof of a causal claim | Separate what happened from what you believe it means |
| Sharing exposes private doubts or another person's beliefs | Keep the original private and choose a small summary, if anything |
| Reflection replaces needed practical or clinical help | Write the concern briefly and seek the relevant support |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **Low vision:** large text, high contrast and generous spacing; no faint decorative labels.
- **Motor or energy limits:** one typed word or a brief dictated line; no ritual action is required.
- **Cognitive load:** one prompt at a time, with “leave open” and “stop” as complete options.
- **Sensory needs:** avoid imposed music, scents, silence, darkness or touch; choose the setting yourself.
- **Language and culture:** use your preferred terms; distinguish a translation from your own paraphrase.
- **Religious trauma or coercion:** use a neutral title, skip triggering prompts and choose support independently.
- **Hearing or speech differences:** reading, typing and silent reflection all work; spoken prayer is not required.

These adaptations are design suggestions, not tested treatments.

## 21. Combining

Keep a passage in a commonplace book and write a response here. Pair an entry with a low-demand check-in if wanted,
but do not infer spiritual commitment from an energy rating. A travel or memory journal may record a ceremony
without claiming to explain its meaning for everyone present.

## 22. Ready-to-use bank

- What felt meaningful or worth sitting with?
- A question I can leave open is…
- Something I did that reflected a value I chose…
- What happened, and what am I making of it today?
- A word from my own tradition or framework that fits, or does not fit…
- A boundary I want to keep around this practice…
- A passage, its source, and my response…
- Nothing to write today.

## 23. Glossary

| Term | Meaning in this guide |
| --- | --- |
| Attribution | Naming where someone else's words or ideas came from |
| Meaning | What the writer finds significant; it need not be shared by others |
| Ritual | A deliberately chosen act or sequence; no supernatural claim is assumed |
| Secular | Not requiring religious belief or affiliation |
| Tradition | A framework or community the writer chooses to name |

## 24. FAQ

**Can I use this without religious belief?** Yes. “Meaning,” “values” or an ordinary heading can replace “spiritual.”

**Do I need an answer?** No. A question and a short body are enough.

**Can I disagree with an earlier entry?** Yes. Add your present view without rewriting the old one as if it never existed.

**Must I share it with a leader, clinician or group?** No. Choose what to disclose and to whom.

**Does this prove that a practice helps?** No. A personal account records an experience, not a causal test.

## 25. For Journalwright Studio

For Jot, adopt a required body with optional tradition, ritual, question and source fields, plain headings and room
for uncertainty. Let the writer choose their language and whether a source is included.

Do not adopt belief scoring, inferred affiliation, spiritual progress charts, default public sharing, prescribed
supernatural interpretations or compulsory gratitude. Keep reflection separate from diagnosis and treatment.

## 26. Open questions

- Does this small recording format help people reflect in the ways they want? Direct evidence is lacking.
- Which terms feel welcoming across different communities without flattening their differences? Ask intended readers.
- When does rereading support reflection, and when does it become a distressing loop? This guide cannot predict it.
- How can source references preserve context across translations and editions? A single field may need a fuller note.

## 27. Further reading

For a particular tradition, choose sources from that tradition and keep their context and permissions. This guide
does not supply a universal reading list. Nearby guides on [prompt journaling](prompt-journaling.md),
[commonplace books](commonplace-book.md) and [low-demand check-ins](low-demand-check-in.md) offer other page structures.
