# Drawing practice log

A drawing practice log is a short record kept after each practice session: what you drew, with what, for how long, which skill you were working on, what worked and what you will try next. It is the first slice of the "Drawing practice" plan in [art-and-junk-journaling.md](art-and-junk-journaling.md) (section 25, d2). It turns a sketchbook into something you can look back through by skill, without streaks or scores. Sources were checked on 2026-10-27. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked **(unconfirmed)**.

## 1. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Creator** | No single creator. Draws on atelier and life-drawing traditions, Kimon Nicolaïdes (*The Natural Way to Draw*, 1941), Betty Edwards (*Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain*, 1979) and the idea of deliberate practice (Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer, 1993) |
| **Year** | The log format here: 2026. Its parts are older |
| **Type** | Practice log / creative skill journal |
| **Time per day** | 1–2 minutes of logging after a session of any length |
| **Time per week** | 2 minutes to look back over the week's `next-try` lines |
| **Cost** | $0 beyond paper and something to draw with |
| **Formats** | Sketchbook margin, index card, `.jot` record, scan or photo attached in a `.jotz` |
| **Best for** | People learning to draw who want to see progress by skill; returning artists; people who lose track of what they practised |
| **Not ideal for** | Drawing purely for relaxation, where any logging feels like homework |
| **Learning curve** | Low |
| **Supplies** | Anything that makes a mark |
| **Official site** | None |
| **Evidence level** | Some (deliberate-practice research and drawing-accuracy research; nothing on this log format) |

## 2. Summary

- One short record per practice session, kept beside the drawing rather than replacing it.
- Each record names the **subject**, **medium** and **focus** (gesture, contour, value, proportion, perspective, form, colour, composition and so on), and optionally the minutes spent, the reference, what worked and what to try next.
- The `next-try` line of one session becomes the starting point of the next, which is the core of deliberate practice: a specific target and feedback.
- References made by other people are credited.
- No streaks, grades or "days missed" counters.

## 3. History and origin

- **1941**: Kimon Nicolaïdes' *The Natural Way to Draw* is published after his death, organised as a schedule of timed exercises (gesture, contour, weight) to be done in order [1].
- **1979**: Betty Edwards publishes *Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain*, built around exercises such as upside-down copying and pre- and post-course self-portraits as a baseline [2]. Its brain-hemisphere framing is not supported by later neuroscience; the exercises and the before-and-after comparison remain widely used.
- **1993**: Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer describe "deliberate practice": effortful practice aimed at a specific weakness, with feedback [3]. A 2019 replication found the effect real but much smaller than first reported [4].
- **2016**: Chamberlain and Wagemans review why most adults draw inaccurately and conclude that what you choose to look at and how you segment a scene matters more than raw perception or motor skill [5].
- **2026**: Jot adds a `drawing-practice` profile so a session can be logged as plain text.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

- **Practise one thing at a time.** A session has a focus. Naming it is the most useful line in the record.
- **Look back by skill, not by date.** "All my value studies" is more useful than "October".
- **Progress is seen, not scored.** Compare drawings to earlier drawings of the same focus. No numeric grade is required or suggested.
- **The drawing is the record.** The log points at it; it does not try to describe it fully.
- **Credit what you learn from.** A photo reference, a master copy or a tutorial carries its creator's name.

## 5. Core components

- **Subject**: what was drawn ("hands holding a mug", "the view from the bus stop").
- **Medium**: what it was drawn with ("graphite 2B", "ballpoint", "tablet, round brush").
- **Focus**: the skill practised. Repeatable.
- **Minutes**: whole minutes actually spent. Optional.
- **Reference**: from life, from a photo, from memory, a master copy, a link. Repeatable.
- **Credit**: who made a reference that is not yours. Repeatable.
- **What worked**: one observation you want to keep.
- **Next try**: one concrete change for next time.
- **Asset and alt**: an optional photo or scan of the piece with a text description.

## 6. Setting it up

1. Choose where the drawing lives (sketchbook, loose paper, tablet).
2. Choose where the log lives. In Jot, open the Methods dialog in `/write/`, search "drawing" and insert the invitation with its record.
3. Make a short list of focus words you care about (see section 22). You can add more any time.
4. Optionally make a baseline: draw your hand, a mug and a chair from life, ten minutes each, and log them. These are the drawings you will revisit.

## 7. Daily practice

There is no required daily rhythm. After any session:

1. Write the subject, medium and focus (30 seconds).
2. Add minutes if you know them. Do not start a timer just for the log.
3. Write one thing that worked and one thing to try next (1 minute).
4. If you photograph the drawing, attach it and write a line of alt text.

## 8. Rhythms

- **Weekly**: read the week's `next-try` lines. Pick one to be next week's focus.
- **Monthly**: gather the month's records by focus and lay the drawings side by side.
- **Quarterly**: redraw the baseline subjects and compare.
- **Missed days**: nothing is recorded. Pick up from the last `next-try`.

## 10. Worked examples

A day (invented content):

```jot
:::record session #draw-2026-10-27
method: drawing-practice@1
subject: Hands holding a mug
medium: Graphite, 2B
focus: gesture
minutes: 20
reference: My own left hand, in a mirror
what-worked: Long lines for the fingers kept them in proportion.
next-try: Draw the mug first, then wrap the hand around it.
:::
```

A session from a photo reference, with the piece attached (inside a `.jotz`):

```jot
:::record session #draw-2026-10-28
method: drawing-practice@1
subject: Harbour cranes at dusk
medium: Ink wash
focus: value
focus: composition
minutes: 35
reference: https://example.org/photos/harbour-cranes
credit: Photo by A. Example, CC BY 4.0
asset: assets/harbour-cranes-study.jpg
alt: Three dark crane silhouettes against a pale sky, with grey water below.
what-worked: Three values only made the shapes read from across the room.
next-try: Keep the lightest value for the sky alone.
relation: continues #draw-2026-10-27
:::
```

A week: five records with focus `gesture` on three days and `value` on two; the weekly review notes that every gesture `next-try` mentioned starting with the largest shape, and makes that next week's focus.

A month: the four quarterly-baseline drawings of a mug, side by side, each with its record.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

- **Timed gesture sets**: one record for a whole set ("30 × 1-minute poses"), not one per drawing.
- **Master copies**: `reference` names the original and `credit` the artist; useful for composition and value studies.
- **Draw-a-day challenges**: log only if you want to. The profile does not count days.
- **Digital practice**: `medium` names the app and brush; attach an exported image.
- **Strand practice**: a long run on one focus (for example a month of perspective) before switching.

## 12. Community practice

Many artists keep practice logs in sketchbooks or on social media, often as dated grids of studies. Online "draw-a-day" challenges are common; some people find them motivating and others find the daily pressure harmful. Life-drawing groups commonly run timed poses of increasing length, following the Nicolaïdes pattern. No personal accounts are described here.

## 13. Official products and formats

Not applicable: there is no official drawing practice log product. Commercial "sketchbook prompt" books exist but are not a log format.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- Any paper and any pencil or pen. Printer paper and a ballpoint are enough.
- A phone camera for optional photos of the drawing.
- A kitchen timer only if you want to time poses; the log does not require one.
- Low-cost tablet styluses work for digital practice.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

- A `.jot` file holds the text record; a `.jotz` package can also hold photos or exports of the drawings.
- A paper sketchbook with a page number in `reference` or the subject line keeps the drawing physical and the log searchable.
- What is lost: texture, scale and pressure in a photo. The log does not claim to reproduce the drawing.

## 16. Evidence and research

- **Deliberate practice** [3]: practice aimed at a specific weakness, with feedback, is associated with skill. The 2019 replication [4] found a substantial but much smaller effect, and talent and other factors matter too. Evidence level for this part: some.
- **Drawing accuracy** [5]: errors in drawing come largely from representational decisions (how a scene is segmented, reliance on schemas), which suggests practising specific ways of looking. This supports logging a named `focus`.
- **Drawing and memory** [6]: drawing something improves memory for it compared with writing it, across ages and regardless of drawing ability. This is about learning content, not about drawing skill.
- No study was found on drawing practice logs themselves. Overall evidence level: **some**, for the parts, not the format.

## 17. Benefits

- A named focus makes practice deliberate (supported in general by [3], [4]).
- A `next-try` line gives the next session a starting point (practitioner report).
- Grouping by focus makes progress visible without numbers (practitioner report).
- Credits keep track of what you learned from (good practice, see [7]).

## 18. Pitfalls

- **Logging becomes the task.** Keep it to the required fields. Partial records are fine.
- **Comparing with other people's work.** Compare with your own earlier drawings of the same focus.
- **Streak pressure.** The profile has no streak field on purpose.
- **Vague focus.** "Got better" is not a focus. Use one of the bank words.
- **Uncredited references.** Add `credit` as you go; it is hard to find later.

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **Vision**: alt text describes each attached image; focus words do not depend on colour.
- **Motor**: large markers, digital drawing with stabilisers, or very short sessions; `minutes` may be 2.
- **Cognitive / executive function**: insert the invitation from the Methods dialog so the fields are already there; fill only what you remember.
- **Energy**: a one-line record (`subject` and `focus`) is a valid partial capture.
- **Sensory**: medium is free text, so dry or quiet media can be preferred without comment.
- **Colour blindness**: value and colour studies can name hues in words.

## 21. Combining

- **Art and junk journaling**: the drawing sits on a journal page; the practice record sits beside it.
- **Focus rounds**: time a session as a round and log the drawing separately.
- **Moodboard and palette**: a colour study can link to the palette it tested with `relation: about #palette-id`.
- **Commonplace book**: keep quotes from drawing books with attribution.

## 22. Ready-to-use bank

Focus words: gesture, contour, blind contour, proportion, measuring, negative space, value, edges, form, light and shadow, perspective, anatomy, drapery, texture, line weight, composition, colour, colour mixing, observation from memory, speed.

Prompts:

- What was the one thing I practised?
- What surprised me in this drawing?
- What would I change if I drew it again tomorrow?
- Which earlier drawing should I compare this with?

Baseline set: your hand, a mug, a chair, a view from a window, a self-portrait. Ten minutes each.

## 23. Glossary

- **Baseline**: a set of drawings made at the start, redrawn later for comparison.
- **Blind contour**: drawing an outline while looking only at the subject.
- **Deliberate practice**: effortful practice aimed at a specific weakness, with feedback.
- **Gesture drawing**: fast drawing of a pose's movement, often 30 seconds to 2 minutes.
- **Master copy**: a study made by copying an existing artwork, credited.
- **Value**: lightness or darkness, independent of hue.

## 24. FAQ

- **Do I have to log every drawing?** No. Log the sessions you want to learn from.
- **Should I give myself a score?** The profile has no score field. Compare drawings instead.
- **Can I use photos from the internet as references?** For private practice, often yes; always credit the creator, and check the licence before sharing the result.
- **Does `minutes` have to be exact?** No. An honest estimate is fine; leave it out if unsure.

## 25. For Journalwright Studio

- **Adopt**: the `drawing-practice@1` profile; a printed "practice" block with subject, medium, focus, worked and next lines below a sketch box; a monthly page grouped by focus.
- **Adopt**: baseline and revisit pages, as planned in the art-journal doc.
- **Don't adopt**: streak counters, star ratings, or "days missed" tallies.
- **Don't adopt**: generated example drawings or AI prompts on printed pages.

## 26. Open questions

- No study was found on whether logging a named focus improves drawing more than practice without logging.
- How often a baseline should be redrawn is a matter of preference; quarterly is a suggestion **(unconfirmed)**.
