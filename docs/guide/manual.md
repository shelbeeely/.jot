# Write your own manual

A journal becomes yours when you decide what it is for and how you use it, and write that down. A short manual does this. It's the
difference between a system you keep and a notebook you decorate: one page that says why you're doing this, what you do, and what you
do when it slips.

It doesn't need to be long or good. A sentence under each heading is enough, and you'll change it as you go.

## The six questions

| Heading | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| **Why I keep this** | What you hope the journal does for you. Not what a journal should do: what you want from this one |
| **What I do** | Daily, weekly, monthly. The template editor writes this part from your template; you add *why* each one is there |
| **My marks** | What `[>]`, `?`, `!` and the others mean *to you*, and when you use them. Leave out the ones you don't use |
| **What I skip on purpose** | What you deliberately don't do, so skipping it is a choice and not a failure |
| **After a gap** | How you start again after days or weeks away, without catching up |
| **When I revise this** | When you'll read the manual again and change it: the start of each month is a good default |

## Making one in the template editor

1. Build your journal's shape first (see [Using the template editor](template-editor.md)).
2. In **Before the days**, press **Add a block** and choose **My manual**.
3. The month's file now starts with your manual. **What I do** lists every block in your template, zone by zone, with a dash after
   each for you to write why it's there:

```jot
#### What I do

[[Written from my template. After each one, why it is there; cross out what I don’t use.]]

Each day:

- Energy (a scale, 1–5, empty to full) — the first thing I fill in, before coffee. A guess is fine.
- Care (boxes: Meds, Water, Outside) — the basics. A ticked box is a win, not a minimum.
- Today (room to write) — one line is enough.
```

To keep the manual as its own file, open **More → Download my manual (.jot)**. It works even if you haven't added the block. Like
every file the editor makes, it carries your template, so opening it in the editor brings the template back.

In the block's options (⚙), **Hints** turns the short note under each heading on or off, and **Title** renames the page.

## Filling it in

Write under each heading in any text editor, on a phone or on InkDeck. A few things that help:

- **Be specific.** "Three tasks a week, never more" is a rule you can keep. "Be more organised" is not.
- **Write the why after the dash.** If you can't say why a block is there, it may not need to be.
- **Make your marks yours.** `[-]` can mean "a decision, not a failure". If you never use `[<]`, say so.
- **Plan for the gap now,** while you're keen. Future you, two weeks behind, will be glad of one kind sentence.
- **Date the last revision** at the bottom, so you can see when the manual and the journal drifted apart.

```jot
#### After a gap

[[How I start again after days away, without catching up.]]

Start with today's date. Don't fill in the missed days. If something from the gap matters, one line under today.
```

## Keeping it with the journal

- **In each month:** with the **My manual** block in **Before the days**, every month you download starts with the manual's
  headings. Copy your answers in, or keep them in one `manual.jot` and let the month's copy be a reminder to reread it.
- **Its own file:** keep `manual.jot` next to your months, on your computer or on InkDeck's card.
- **Revise it** when you change the template. The **What I do** part is rewritten from the template each time, so the two stay in step.
